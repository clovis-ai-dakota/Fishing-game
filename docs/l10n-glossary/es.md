# Spanish glossary (values-es)

Neutral Spanish for Spain and Latin America (the store listing is es-ES). Use these words on every
screen, so the tour, the HUD, the shop and the store listing say the same thing.

## Register and style

- **Tú** for the player (as in the store listing): "Arrastra", "Tu cuerda", "¿Quieres...?".
- **No vosotros and no ustedes**: write "ambos jugadores pescan", "la amistad quedará confirmada",
  never "pescáis" or "pescan ustedes" addressed to the players.
- **Gender-neutral where it costs nothing**: "Te damos la bienvenida", "La victoria será para tu
  rival", "si estás en línea" (not "conectado"), "Tu membresía es de por vida" (not "socio"). Where
  Spanish has no neutral word, use the generic masculine ("pescadores", "Jugador 1", "Fundador").
- **Neutral vocabulary**: "teléfono" (not "móvil" or "celular"), "app", "en línea".
- **Spanish punctuation**: ¿...? and ¡...!. A no-break space (U+00A0) before `%` ("45 %",
  "-25 %", "Top 10 %"). Units with a space: "120 m", "10 kg", "1.250 pts". Compact chips: "5 mil",
  "1,5 M".
- **Capitals**: sentence case for labels and modes ("Captura pesada", "Pase de temporada"). Place
  names (zones, themes, boats, seasons) capitalise each main word ("Fosa de Medianoche",
  "Bosque de Algas"). Lantern is an item name: always "Farol" / "Faroles" with a capital.

## Names inside sentences

Spanish doesn't inflect names, but articles and agreement still get in the way:

- **Zones** after "la zona": "Captura un pez en la zona Fosa de Medianoche".
- **Club names** after "club": "¡Te damos la bienvenida al club Salty Herons!".
- **Item, mode and option names** after a colon or in parentheses: "¡Regalo enviado a Ana: Bolsa
  de Faroles!", "Esta opción (Franja deportiva) cuesta...", "Bloqueado: Cuadros", "te desafía:
  Captura pesada".
- **Player names** go after a preposition or a colon ("Turno de Ana: pásale el teléfono").
  `shop_gift_from_you` is "ti", because it is only ever used after "Regalo de".
- **Sparkling** goes after the name: "Sun Bream brillante" ("brillante" never changes for gender).

## Plurals

Spanish uses `one`, `many` and `other`. `many` covers exact millions, where Spanish puts "de"
before the noun, so it repeats `other` with "de": "%d de días", "%d de Faroles". Units stay
unchanged ("%d kg de peces"). Write all three forms even when they are identical.
