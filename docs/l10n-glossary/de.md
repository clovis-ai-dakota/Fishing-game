# German glossary (values-de)

The choices behind `app/src/main/res/values-de/`, so every screen (and the store listing in
`docs/store/listings/de-DE/`) uses the same words. See the shared glossary in
[`docs/localization.md`](../localization.md), section 11.

## Register and style

- **Informal "du"**, as in the store listing. Lower case (`du`, `dein`) inside sentences; capital
  only at the start of a sentence, also after a colon when a full sentence follows ("Titel: Du
  hast …"). The player's own name in a battle is "Du".
- Imperatives in the short colloquial form, like the listing: "Fang", "Lenk", "Zieh", "Hol",
  "Wirf … aus".
- Quotation marks „…“. Labels of buttons, tabs and pages are quoted in running text: im Tab
  „Angeln“, der Button „Auswerfen“, die Seite „Seil“.
- Percent with a no-break space: `10&#160;%` (`%1$d&#160;%%` with arguments).
- Units keep their symbols with a space: `m`, `kg`, `m/s`. Durations use the symbols `d`, `h`,
  `min` (no full stop, so nothing doubles up at the end of a sentence).
- Multiplication sign as in English: "3× so viel", "×1,6".
- Ellipsis is the single character "…".
- Generic masculine for people ("Angler", "Spieler", "Besitzer", "Gründer"), as most German games
  do; where it was easy, neutral wording ("die andere Person", "sobald sie angenommen wird").

## Names in sentences

German cases change articles and adjective endings, so a name passed as an argument always sits
where no grammar touches it:

- **After a colon or in parentheses**: "Zu schwer: %1$s", "Fang %1$d Fische in dieser Zone: %2$s.",
  "Fang einen Fisch (%1$s)", "Freischalten: %1$s?", "Gesperrt: %1$s", "Angeln lassen: %1$s",
  "%1$s: Auswerfen!", "Herausfordern: %1$s", "%1$s fordert dich heraus: %2$s.".
- **Zones and modes** never follow a preposition that would need an article; the tour writes them
  in itself and inflects them there ("Erreiche die Tiefseeebene").
- **Species** (English names, no gender) after a colon or in parentheses: "Funkelnd: %1$s",
  "✦ %1$s (funkelnd)", "Fang diesen Riesen …: %1$s.".
- **Club and item names** in „…“ after a noun: "im Club „%1$s“", "Titel „%1$s“", "Boot „%1$s“",
  "Design „%1$s“". Grant lists after "Du bekommst:" are written in the nominative, with neuter or
  numbered phrases ("ein Saisonabzeichen", "1 Serienschild") so they also read right as objects.
- **Points with their unit** ("2.400 Punkte") never follow a dative preposition ("mit", "von"),
  which would need "Punkten": "Du hast den 3. Platz von 40 belegt (2.400 Punkte)".
- **Ranks** come as ordinals ("3."): "den %1$s Platz", "%1$s von %2$s"; cardinal ranks use "Platz %2$s".
- The gift sender `shop_gift_from_you` is "dir", because it only appears after "Geschenk von".

## Plurals

German has `one` and `other`. Where the counted noun has no plural or the sentence avoids it,
both forms are the same ("%d Funkelköder", "%d-mal pro Tag", "Serie: %d Tage" vs "Tag"). Swaps
are counted as "%d-mal" (no plural noun for "Tausch"). Dative plurals add -n where the noun
follows "mit"/"von" ("mit %d Versuchen", "Aquarium mit %d Fischen", "%1$d von %2$d Mitgliedern").
